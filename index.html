About me

Computational Mathematics graduate from Universitat Jaume I, finishing an Erasmus Mundus MSc in Geospatial Technologies across Lisbon, Münster and Castellón.

What I enjoy most is taking something from theory and making it work on real data, especially when the result matters to someone outside a paper. Right now that means data science: finding the patterns in a dataset that nobody had spotted yet, and turning them into something people can use.

In the last year I've lived in Lisbon, Münster and Castellón, and moving that often taught me to settle in fast and figure things out on the go. I speak Spanish, Catalan and English, and I get by in French and Portuguese.

Before all of that, I spent two and a half years as a youth activity instructor. I think everyone should have a job taking care of people at least once. It shows you how to treat others when they depend on you, and that has stayed with me more than any course.

I also helped organise GeoMundus 2026, an international conference run by students. Coordinating speakers, sponsors and venues with a group of friends showed me how much I enjoy building something with other people.

I work best where there's trust and people share ideas openly. Outside work, most of my time goes to my family and friends.

Projects

01

Lightning that follows lightning

Lightning doesn't fall at random. When one strike hits, more tend to follow nearby and soon after, a bit like aftershocks after an earthquake. In my master's thesis I'm measuring how much of that clustering comes from the storm itself (heat, humidity, mountains) and how much is one strike making the next one more likely. The data: twenty years of strikes over two regions of southern France.

Master's thesis, in progress, 2026 to 2027

02

Community Hazard Response Platform

When a disaster hits a neighbourhood, like a flood or a wildfire, much of the first help comes from the people living next door. But those who need help and those who can give it often don't know about each other. We built a platform where residents report what they need, volunteers say what they can offer, and a map-aware database pairs each request with the nearest helper.

Group project, NOVA IMS Lisbon, 2026

03

Mapping the Valencia flood from space

In October 2024 a storm flooded large parts of Valencia. I used satellite images to map which areas were under water. The tricky part: the water was so full of mud that the usual methods didn't recognise it as water. Adding a measure that picks up mud fixed it, and it became one of the most useful signals for the model.

Team project, University of Münster, 2026
