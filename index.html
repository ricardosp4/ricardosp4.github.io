About

Computational Mathematics graduate from Universitat Jaume I, currently finishing an Erasmus Mundus MSc in Geospatial Technologies across NOVA IMS (Lisbon), the University of Münster and Universitat Jaume I.

My work moves between mathematical theory and applied implementation. I've optimised low-level memory algorithms for scientific data compression, analysed clinical datasets to flag hospital risk factors, and mapped floods from satellite imagery with machine learning. For my thesis, supervised by Jorge Mateu and Edzer Pebesma, I'm modelling lightning strikes in southern France as a self-exciting point process. I work comfortably in Python and R, and I like building tools other people can actually use, not just papers that describe them.

In the last year I've lived in Lisbon, Münster and Castellón, and moving that often taught me to settle in fast and figure things out on the go. I speak Spanish, Catalan and English, and I get by in French and Portuguese.

Before all of that, I spent two and a half years as a youth activity instructor. I think everyone should have a job taking care of people at least once. It shows you how to treat others when they depend on you, and that has stayed with me more than any course.

I also helped organise GeoMundus 2026, an international conference run by students. Coordinating speakers, sponsors and venues with a group of friends showed me how much I enjoy building something with other people.

I work best where there's trust and people share ideas openly. Outside work, most of my time goes to my family and friends.

Projects
R package

stevents

An open-source R package for spatio-temporal event analysis, built from scratch around a vectorised Hawkes-process intensity kernel. Includes S3 classes for event sets and spatial grids, neighbourhood and counting utilities, two vignettes, bundled real-world datasets, and a full testthat suite with CI.

  • Core method Vectorised O(n²) exponential decay intensity kernel
  • Coverage 24 unit tests, GitHub Actions CI
  • Datasets Venezuela 2026 · Amatrice 2016

Machine learning · Remote sensing

Valencia flood mapping

Random Forest classification of the flood extent left by the October 2024 DANA in Valencia, combining Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 optical data in Google Earth Engine. The floodwater was full of sediment, so standard water indices missed much of it; adding a mud-sensitive index fixed that, and it came out as the second most important predictor.

  • Features 35-band stack: spectral indices, GLCM texture, morphological filters
  • Validation 70/30 split, accuracy, kappa, per-class F1
  • Context Team of three, University of Münster, 2026

In progress · Thesis

Lightning as a self-exciting process

Do lightning strikes trigger more strikes nearby once you account for the weather and the terrain? Two decades of strike data from Ardèche and Drôme, modelled as a spatio-temporal Hawkes process with ERA5 covariates (CAPE, mid-level humidity, wind shear) and elevation.

  • Methods Maximum likelihood · EMbru (EM + INLA) · neural point process
  • Status Ongoing, Sep 2026 to Feb 2027
